THREE-MINUTE ORAL UPDATE — 30 OCTOBER 2026

My project is supervised binary image classification for industrial motor defects.
The classifier takes one motor image, not a pair of images, and outputs one of two classes: normal or defective.

The source is the public M2AD Motor dataset. It was originally designed for anomaly detection, so I am adapting it for supervised two-class classification.
I have a small, preliminary development sample with 70 training images and 29 validation images. I know that physical specimen independence still needs verification.

My baseline is ResNet-18 initialized from ImageNet and fine-tuned for two classes with cross-entropy loss. It has run successfully. Its exploratory validation accuracy is 96.55 percent and macro-F1 is approximately 0.965. These are validation results, not final independent test accuracy.

I have prepared two papers. My primary candidate is PolyLoss, published at ICLR 2022. The idea is to add a small polynomial term to ordinary cross entropy, changing only the loss while keeping the ResNet-18 architecture the same.
My alternative is a 2024 preprint on Next Generation Loss functions, which explores new classification losses discovered by genetic programming. It is interesting but more complex to reproduce.

My experimental question is whether Poly-1 changes macro-F1 and defect recall under a controlled comparison. I will use the same split, initialization, optimizer, preprocessing and training budget for both methods. I will also report the confusion matrix, errors, and data-leakage limitations.

The Poly-1 code and initial verification tests are ready, but the matched training is not complete because most source images must be restored in a network-enabled environment.

I would like to confirm that this supervised M2AD binary formulation and the PolyLoss paper are acceptable for the course.
